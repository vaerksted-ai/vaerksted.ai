What Skjald does

A recording is not meeting notes, and a transcript is not much better. Both tell you what was said. What people come back for is what was decided, who does what, and who said it. Skjald records the meeting, transcribes it, and writes up those three things: the decisions, the owners and the speakers.

It is built for professionals who want that write-up without giving up control of the conversation. That is a choice about where the work happens, which is the next section.

Where the work happens

Recording, transcription and the written outcome run on your own device by default. That covers all three steps, not only the recording.

There is one exception, and it sits next to the claim on purpose. Audio goes to Skjald Cloud only on a paid plan, when you pick Cloud for a meeting. Skjald Cloud is optional and paid, and its processing is not limited to the EU.

That is the whole boundary: where the work happens by default, and the one case where audio goes to the cloud. Decide for yourself with that in front of you.

Three questions for any "on-device" notetaker

The phrase covers a lot of different products. Before you rely on it, ask:

  1. Which steps run on the device? Recording, transcription and the written outcome are three separate steps. Skjald runs all three on your device by default.
  2. What is the default, and what has to happen for audio to leave? For Skjald, audio goes to Skjald Cloud only on a paid plan, when you pick Cloud for a meeting.
  3. Where is the optional cloud path processed? For Skjald Cloud, processing is not limited to the EU.

A tool that answers the first question and skips the other two has told you less than it seems.

Getting the notes out

Skjald hands the notes on in the formats people already use: mail, PDF, Word, Markdown, or a webhook to an address you enter.

  • Mail. Skjald opens a draft in your mail app. You read it and send it. Skjald does not send the mail itself.
  • PDF and Word. For notes that go to people who just need to read them.
  • Markdown. For notes that go into your own notes or documents.
  • Webhook. For sending the notes to an address you enter, so another system can pick them up.
