Why most AI training doesn't change how a product team works

Much of what is on offer teaches a tool. People leave knowing how to write a prompt, then go back to writing specs the way they always did. The work that decides whether a product team is good, finding the real problem, deciding what not to build, writing something engineers can act on, never got practised with AI on real material.

We start from the other end. We take the notes, roadmap and specs your team already has, work through them with AI in the room, and finish with a playbook your team keeps using after we leave. The measure is simple: next sprint, does the team do a part of its job differently?

What a full-day team workshop looks like

A day follows the work your team does, not a slide deck. This is the shape; we adjust it to your team before the day.

Morning

  1. Start from your work. You bring a real discovery question, a roadmap and a spec in progress. We agree what "better" would look like for this team, so there is something to check against later.
  2. Discovery with AI. Turn interview notes and support conversations into themes, problem statements and open questions. We look hard at where the output is wrong, because that is where the team learns what to trust.

Afternoon

  1. Specs and prioritisation. Draft and tighten specs, test them against edge cases, and pressure-test the ranking of your backlog. The team decides what stays with a person.
  2. Roadmap. Use AI to challenge the roadmap you brought: assumptions, gaps, and what you would drop.
  3. Build your library. Turn what worked into prompts and reusable skills, write the first version of the playbook, and name who owns it.

We close by agreeing a 30-day check-in.

Where AI helps product work, and where it misleads

The training spends as much time on the failures as on the wins, because a team that only sees the wins stops checking.

  • Discovery. AI is quick at clustering interview notes and surfacing themes. It also smooths over the odd, specific comment that often matters most. We teach the team to read the source quotes behind every theme.
  • Specs. A model will write a fluent spec for a problem nobody has confirmed. We use it to find missing edge cases and unstated assumptions, not to replace the thinking about the problem.
  • Prioritisation. AI can apply your criteria consistently across a long backlog. It cannot tell you whether the criteria are right. That stays a team decision.
  • Roadmaps. Asking AI to argue against your roadmap exposes weak assumptions cheaply. Asking it to write the roadmap gives you an average of everyone else's.

Before the day: what to bring

A workshop on your own material only works if the material is in the room. We agree the list with you beforehand. Expect to bring recent discovery notes or interview recordings, the current roadmap, one or two specs in progress, and the list of AI tools your team already uses. Anything sensitive stays under your own rules, and we decide together what is safe to put in front of a model.

What your team takes away

  • A written playbook. Which steps of discovery, specs, roadmap and prioritisation you hand to AI, which you check, and which stay with a person.
  • A prompt and skill library built on your own work. Not generic templates. Things your team has already tried on its material and kept.
  • A 30-day follow-up. We look at what stuck and what did not, and fix the playbook.

FAQ

How to learn about AI as a product manager?

Learn it on your own work. Pick a real task, such as turning interview notes into a problem statement or a draft spec into edge-case tests, do it with AI, and compare the result with how you did it before. A course helps most when it runs on your material and ends with habits your team keeps. That is how our workshops are built.

Can AI do product management?

It can do parts of it well: summarising research, drafting specs, spotting gaps in a plan. It cannot own the decision about what to build and why. Good training teaches a team which steps to hand over, which to check and which to keep. We draw that line with your team, using your own discovery, roadmap and prioritisation work.

What does AI training for product teams include?

Hands-on work on your team's own material across discovery, specs, roadmap and prioritisation. You leave with a written playbook, a prompt and skill library built from your work, and a 30-day follow-up. It does not include a certificate.

What are the best AI courses for product managers?

The best AI course is the kind after which your team works differently. Check four things: it runs on your own discovery notes, roadmap and specs; it shows you where AI gets it wrong; it ends with a playbook your team keeps; and someone looks at what stuck after a month. A certificate tells you far less than those four.
